Chordata listens to the belts with an accelerometer on the toolhead (an ADXL345 or LIS2DW board, or a probe with one built in). Add one and [resonance_tester] to printer.cfg.

How: Measuring Resonances

Made for CoreXY printers

On a CoreXY printer both belts pull the toolhead, so they must match. This printer's kinematics: .

How it works

  1. Set the target and park the toolhead at your tensioning spot.
  2. Tap one spot on the toolhead, 3 times.
  3. Pluck either belt mid-span and read the number.
  4. Turn a tensioner a little, pluck again, watch the history.
  5. Bring both belts to the target, and to each other.
  6. Double tap the toolhead, or Reset history, to start the history over.
  7. Done, then Close Chordata (fans come back on).

Red: hands off. Yellow: tap the toolhead. Green: pluck. Chordata only moves the toolhead when you press a button.

More
  • A plucked belt rings like a guitar string; the accelerometer on the toolhead hears it. Tension goes with the frequency squared: 1% in Hz is 2% in tension. On CoreXY both belts pull on each other, so they must match.
  • Use the target your printer's guide gives for the stretch of belt it says to pluck. Turn off loud fans if you can.
  • Move the toolhead with the arrows or by typing X and Y, and save it as the belt tensioning position; next time, Go to belt tensioning position takes it there.
  • The setup shows your accelerometer and the frequencies it hears reliably (from its datasheet and the rate Klipper sets). A target outside that range is shown in red: that sensor can't measure it.
  • The taps teach Chordata how the toolhead rings by itself, so a knock is never taken for a belt. Tap only one solid spot (another spot rings differently); an X doesn't count, keep tapping until 3 check marks. A stiff toolhead that rings nothing near the belts has nothing to learn. The taps run at every start (work on the toolhead changes how it rings), after one background recording while the screen is red: hands off then. A toolhead that rings long: pluck firmly, hands off the toolhead while plucking.
  • Tuner Mode stops 60 seconds after your last pluck, or press Done. Pluck evenly, the same way each time, not too hard. The phone beeps when it is ready, and the box around the number lights up for each pluck heard.
  • The history keeps this session's plucks (the last 100), newest at the top; refused ones are grey, with why. A new session, Close Chordata and Reset history clear it.
  • A pluck is on target within 0.25 Hz. Only clear plucks count: 12 dB over the background noise and not a knock on the toolhead.
  • Belt tension: give the span you pluck (at the belt tensioning position, from where the belt leaves one pulley to where it meets the next one or the clamp) and the belt's weight per metre under Belt on Set Target Frequency, and the tension shows under the Hz, in N or lbf. The target can then be typed in Hz, N or lbf; a tension is set as its Hz (to 0.1 Hz). Gates-Unitta 2GT (most printers' belts) weighs 1.3 g/m per mm of width: 6 mm 7.8 g/m, 9 mm 11.7 g/m; Gates GT2 / GT3 1.4: 6 mm 8.4 g/m, 9 mm 12.6 g/m, 12 mm 16.8 g/m. Other makes differ: type theirs under Other. Tension goes with the span squared: 3% off on the span is 6% off in tension, so the N are good to about 15-20%, and read a little high on short spans. Your two belts, same span and same belt, compare as well as their Hz do.
  • A fan hums at one steady level; a pluck starts loud and dies away. A hum that starts after the background was recorded is left out, and if it sits on the belt's note Chordata says so.

Tension needs the span

A target in N or lbf needs the span you pluck and the belt. Set them first, or keep the target in Hz.
